Every network team hears the same lines in meetings. "The change is low risk." "We're fully redundant." "It's up, so it's working." "We need to replace all of it." Each line is usually said with confidence and rarely shown with evidence.

The RKR Networks Podcast takes one of those lines per episode and tests it. A network lead sits in the customer's chair and asks the questions they would ask in a real meeting: sceptical, practical and short on patience for slides. An RKR technical marketing engineer answers from what recorded demos actually showed, on made-up example sites, in a synthetic twin.

Two things to know before you press play. Everything discussed comes from recorded demos on made-up sites, not from a customer network. And both voices are AI-generated. The questions and the evidence are the point.

## The format, briefly

Each episode follows the same shape, so you can jump in anywhere:

1. **The claim.** The network lead opens with a line heard in real meetings.
2. **The scenario.** A made-up site is set up: a building, a campus, a warehouse or a data hall, with its switches, access points, clients and power.
3. **The test.** Something is changed or failed in the synthetic twin, and the result is read out: who is affected, what moved, what stayed up.
4. **The pushback.** The network lead challenges the result. What about the second fault? What about the old gear? What about the users who are connected but unhappy?
5. **The takeaway.** One practical thing to do on Monday morning.

Each episode stays on one claim from start to finish.

## Who it is for

The show is for people who own a network and answer for it: network leads, infrastructure managers, IT directors, data center operators and the engineers who get the 2 AM call. It assumes you know what a VLAN is but does not assume you have time to read a vendor white paper. If a term is new, the [glossary](/glossary/) has a short definition.
